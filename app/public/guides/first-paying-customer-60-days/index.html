How to Get Your First Paying SaaS Customer in 60 Days

Most SaaS founders spend months building features nobody asked for. The 60-day framework flips this: ship a minimal product by day 14, charge from day 1, and iterate on real feedback. Here is the exact sequence.

Days 1-7: Pick one problem, one customer

Find one person who will pay you to solve one problem. Not a market, not a segment — one person. Ask: "What would you pay to have this solved by Friday?" If they cannot name a number, find someone else. The first customer is a co-designer, not a user.

Days 8-14: Ship the minimum thing they pay for

Not an MVP with 14 features. The smallest thing they will pay for. A Google Sheet with a formula. A Notion template. A one-page web app. If it takes more than 7 days to build, you are building too much. Charge from day 1 — free users give polite feedback; paying customers give useful feedback.

Days 15-30: Find 5 more customers like the first

Ask customer 1: "Who else has this problem?" Get three names. Reach out: "X said you might have this problem too. I built something that solved it for them. Want to see it?" This is warmer than cold outreach and faster than content marketing. Your first 10 customers come from your first customer's network.

Days 31-45: Build the pricing page you were afraid to build

Put a price on the page. Not "Contact us" — an actual number. If you are scared to publish the price, the price is too high. If nobody buys, the product is wrong, not the price. A pricing page with one tier and a Stripe link converts better than a beautiful page with 4 tiers and no checkout.

Days 46-60: Launch where your customers already are

Do not launch on Product Hunt. Launch in the Slack group, Discord, or subreddit where your first 10 customers hang out. Post: "I built X. My first 10 customers pay $Y/mo. Here is what I learned." Honest launch posts outperform polished launches. The goal is 10 more paying customers from the launch, not 1,000 upvotes.
