How do you get your first paying customer for a SaaS product

Bottom Line Getting your first paying customer requires validating the problem first (not the solution), finding where your customers already gather, offering a compelling early-adopter deal, and delivering exceptional onboarding — most founders fail because they build before validating.

Validate Before You Build

The #1 mistake is building a product before confirming someone will pay for it. Run 20-30 customer discovery interviews. If you can't get 5 people to say 'I would pay for this' and give you their email, don't write a line of code.

Find Where Your Customers Gather

Your first customers are in specific communities: Reddit subreddits, LinkedIn groups, Slack/Discord communities, industry forums, and Twitter/X. Don't run ads — engage authentically, help people, and mention your solution only when it's directly relevant.

The Early-Adopter Offer

Your first 10 customers should get a 'founding member' deal: significant discount (50-70% off), lifetime pricing lock, direct access to the founder, and input on the product roadmap. In exchange, they provide testimonials, referrals, and product feedback.

Exceptional Onboarding

Your first customers need white-glove onboarding. Personally set up their account, import their data, and schedule a 30-minute kickoff call. A successfully onboarded first customer becomes your best source of referrals and case studies.

Key Data Points

  • 20-30 customer interviews before building
  • 50-70% founding member discount
  • White-glove onboarding

Frequently Asked Questions

  • How long does it take to get the first customer?

    The median time is 4-8 weeks from starting customer discovery, but it varies enormously. Founders who validate first and sell manually (not through ads) typically find their first customer faster than those who build first and market later.
