# Founder VSL Script — Funnel Hub Homepage

**Length:** 3 to 5 minutes spoken (target 3:45).
**Voice:** Reluctant Hero. Scars, not certificates.
**Replaces:** the "Six-line intro video placeholder" block in workbook 07 §5.
**Anchor structure:** per workbook 07 §5 "Lite VSL" — six-line intro (workbook 01 §6 Beat 2) → Big Domino (workbook 07 §1) → disqualifying line (workbook 01 §6 Beat 5) → 60-day guarantee → CTA.
**Rule, non-negotiable:** look-at-camera moments are for the painful admissions, not the hype lines. The founder confesses to the lens. The founder pitches to the side.

---

## 1) The script (~340 words spoken)

**[Beat 1 — Opening confession. ~50 seconds. Frame: medium close-up. Camera position: slightly off-axis, founder looking PAST the lens to start, then turning into the lens at "the shipping part felt like magic."]**

I'm a marketer. I'm an operator. [pause] I have never written a line of production code in my life.

For most of my life, that closed a door.

Then in 2026, Lovable and Claude opened it. [look at camera] And I shipped real AI products in a few weeks.

The shipping part felt like magic.

[beat]

What came after did not.

---

**[Beat 2 — The flat line. ~45 seconds. Cut to: Stripe dashboard B-roll, no commentary needed. Then back to founder. Founder is NOT energetic here. The voice is matter-of-fact, tired honesty.]**

I'd launch. I'd open Stripe. And I'd watch a line lie flat.

Twelve users. Two paying. A handful of comments that said "this is awesome" from people who'd never reach for a card.

I told myself it was the product. I told myself the funnel was leaking.

[look at camera, hold the look]

I went embarrassingly deep into SEO. AEO. GEO. I got good at being found.

The truth I would not say out loud, for almost a year, was that learning more about traffic wasn't solving my problem.

It was a respectable way of never looking at the flat line.

---

**[Beat 3 — The break. ~40 seconds. This is the emotional pivot. Founder is fully on-camera here. Slight zoom in over the line "that is you. He is describing you."]**

What finally broke me wasn't the dashboard.

[pause]

It was sitting with more than ten other founders. [beat] And hearing my own story back. Every single time.

Halfway through call six, I had to mute. Get up. Walk around the room.

A small cold voice said: [look at camera, slow] that is you. He is describing you.

That was the night I sat down to write the offer for this product, and found nothing on the page.

I'd been building beautiful things for no one in particular and acting surprised when no one paid.

---

**[Beat 3.5 — Quiet addendum to the break. ~15 seconds. Same setup as Beat 3. Eyes off camera, half-mumble. NOT a confession beat — more like a footnote spoken out loud. Inserted per the cross-section overlay (workbook 01 §6 backstory + state.json attractive_character.locked.cross_section_frame). The point of the beat is to flag the skill-mix as the credibility wedge WITHOUT breaking Reluctant Hero voice. Founder must sound like he is admitting something, not selling it.]**

What also became clear that night was that I'd been pretending I was one thing or the other. Just-a-marketer. Just-a-builder.

[beat]

I'm both. In one head. By accident. Almost nobody in this niche is. And that's the only reason I'm trying to build a product about this work instead of writing another thread about it.

---

**[Beat 4 — The Big Domino. ~35 seconds. Founder steps back from confession. Voice firms up. Camera goes slightly wider. No longer looking-into-lens — looking just to the side, like he's making the argument to a peer, not selling.]**

So here is what I'd say to you, if you're on the other side of that flat Stripe line right now.

Your first paying customer is reachable in sixty days.

Not through another feature. Not through more traffic.

Through the one piece of work you were probably taught to skip — name one real person, write one real promise, send one real message — and a tool that refuses to let you skip it.

---

**[Beat 5 — Disqualifier + Guarantee. ~40 seconds. Look back at camera for the disqualifier. Direct. Almost cold. Then soften slightly for the guarantee.]**

[look at camera]

This is not for you if you haven't shipped anything yet. Go ship first. Come back when your Stripe is flat.

[beat]

If you've shipped, here's what I'll put in writing.

Run the playbook I built. Do the work it tracks. If sixty days pass and your Stripe shows no new paying customer, you get both months back. In full. Ninety-eight dollars.

The risk is mine.

---

**[Beat 6 — The CTA. ~20 seconds. Soft. Founder smiles slightly here, the only smile in the video. Last frame: founder looks at camera, says one sentence, holds the look for a beat before cut.]**

You already did the hard part. You built something real.

[look at camera, smile small]

Get your free diagnosis below. Nine minutes. It tells you the truth, even if the truth is hard.

[hold, cut]

## 2) B-roll suggestions (intercut against the script above)

These are not montage filler. Each one is anchored to a specific beat. The B-roll does work the script can't do — it shows what the founder is describing without him having to spell it out.

| # | Shot | Cuts in at | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Stripe dashboard, flat line.** Real screenshot of an actual flat-line month, blurred dollar amounts. Hold for 3 seconds. | Beat 2, over the line "I'd watch a line lie flat." | Shows the pain visually. The viewer's own flat line flashes in their mind. |
| **2** | **Hands on keyboard, typing.** Close-up. Generic, but specifically the moment of late-night "tweaking." Soft warm light. | Beat 2, over "I told myself the funnel was leaking." | Visualizes the avoidance loop without naming it. |
| **3** | **Screenshot pan: Lovable interface, then Claude interface, then a vibe-coded product preview.** 2 seconds each, smooth pan. | Beat 1, over "Lovable and Claude opened it." | Grounds the AI-builder identity. Marco sees his own tool stack on screen. |
| **4** | **Notebook page with offer scribbles.** Real handwriting. Crossed-out lines, arrows, then one circled sentence. | Beat 3, over "found nothing on the page." | Shows the blank-offer-page parable visually, without him narrating it explicitly. |
| **5** | **Wide shot of an empty room, slow zoom, single chair.** | Beat 3, over the "walk around the room" line. | Visualizes the moment of self-recognition without showing the founder doing it on camera (which would feel staged). |
| **6** | **The Playbook interface — Step 5 outreach screen with a "verified" checkmark animating in.** 2-3 seconds, clean. | Beat 4, over "a tool that refuses to let you skip it." | Shows the product mechanically delivering on what the script claims. Concrete proof. |
| **7** | **Stripe dashboard, but this time with one new charge appearing.** 2-3 second push-in on the new line. | Beat 5, over "Stripe shows no new paying customer." | The promise made literal. Last B-roll image the viewer carries away. |

**B-roll rule:** never cut to B-roll on a confession beat. The confession needs the face. B-roll exists for the mechanical / product / proof beats. Keep the painful moments (Beats 1, 3, 5 disqualifier) on the founder's face uncut.

## 3) Recording checklist

Six items. Run through this before hitting record.

1. **Microphone:** lavalier (Rode Wireless Go II or equivalent) clipped at the sternum, not at the collar. Built-in camera mic is a no — viewers register the cheapness in 4 seconds and bail. Aim for under -12 dB peak.
2. **Lighting:** key light at 45 degrees off-camera-left, soft (umbrella or large softbox). No on-camera ring light — the catch-light in the eyes from a ring is recognizable as "selling to me" and breaks the Reluctant Hero frame. Window light during golden hour is acceptable substitute.
3. **Framing:** medium close-up. Founder's eye line at the top third of the frame, headroom minimal. Camera at eye level — never below (gives "lecturing" energy) or above (gives "small" energy).
4. **Background:** real, lived-in, slightly imperfect. A bookshelf with actual books, or a window with daylight, or a textured wall. NOT a blurred bokeh "creator" background, NOT a green-screen office, NOT a "studio." The Reluctant Hero records from a real room.
5. **Wardrobe:** what the founder actually wears on a Tuesday. Plain crew or solid shirt, no logos, no fresh haircut, no makeup beyond the bare minimum to handle camera glare. The viewer should think "this could be me on a Tuesday."
6. **Multiple takes per beat:** record each beat 3-4 times. Best take per beat in the cut. NEVER record the whole thing top-to-bottom on take one and call it done — the painful beats need 2-3 attempts to land naturally. The confession in Beat 3 ("that is you, he is describing you") almost always needs take 4 or later to feel real instead of rehearsed.

## Production notes

- **Master file:** record at 4K, edit at 1080p, export at 1080p for the funnel hub embed. The 4K master gives you flexibility to crop into the three cuts above without losing resolution.
- **Color grade:** flat, slightly desaturated. NOT the warm-and-glossy creator look. The Reluctant Hero voice is undermined by an overly-polished color grade.
- **Music:** none. Silence between beats. Music in this video would feel manipulative — the Reluctant Hero doesn't score his own confessions.
- **End card:** static frame for 4 seconds after the cut. White background, dark text: "Get your free diagnosis: unlocksaas.com/diagnostic". One CTA. Nothing else.
- **A/B test framing for Week 2:** record an alternate take of Beat 4 (Big Domino) with slightly more energy. Test against the calmer version. Avatar is a skeptic — calmer probably wins, but verify.
