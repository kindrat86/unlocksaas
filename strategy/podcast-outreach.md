**Source list:** workbook 08 §2 Category 3 (15 podcast candidates).

## Ranking rationale (read first)

The instinct is to pitch the biggest podcast first. Wrong move. Per workbook 08 §4, the pre-pitch warm-up + the trade-for-the-host matters more than reach. Big shows ignore cold pitches from people without proof. Small-to-mid shows with high avatar density convert.

**Ranking order:** Bootstrapped Founder → Software Social → Indie Hackers → Build Your SaaS → Startup Ideas with Greg Isenberg.

Bootstrapped Founder is #1 because Arvid Kahl's audience IS Marco. He's been preaching customer-centricity to indie hackers for years. The trade is symmetrical — Maryan's story validates his thesis with a fresh proof point and a new generation of builders (AI-shipped).

Greg Isenberg's show is #5 — biggest pull, weakest fit and longest warm-up distance. He'll be a Month 3 pitch, not Week 1.

## Pre-Pitch Warm-Up

The pitch is not the first interaction. The pitch is the third or fourth. Per workbook 08 §4, "work your way in" before "buy your way in" — and the same logic applies to attention.

### Universal 3 actions before sending ANY pitch

1. **Substantive comment on the host's last 2-3 pieces of content** (newsletter editions, podcast clips, tweets, blog posts). Not "great post." A specific paragraph extending a thought they made, ideally with a counterpoint they'd find interesting. Track in the Hosting-Reciprocity Tracker below.
2. **Cite the host publicly** (on X / IH / Reddit) at least once with substance — quote a specific frame, credit them, extend it with your own experience. They'll see it eventually. If they don't, the citation still does the work of seasoning your authority for when the pitch lands.
3. **Send 1 founder you respect** to the host's newsletter / podcast with a "you'd like this" intro. Distribution favors come back. If it's a small list, do it visibly (mention it in the pitch). If it's a single intro, do it quietly.

## Hosting-Reciprocity Tracker

The trade matters more than the pitch. Per workbook 08 §4, every Dream 100 engagement should have a "what does UnlockSaaS offer in exchange" answer ready BEFORE the first message. Vague offers ("I'll plug you") are forgettable. Concrete offers compound.

| Podcast | Concrete trade UnlockSaaS offers | Who initiates |
|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrapped Founder** | Co-branded "Avoidance Loop Self-Check" PDF — Arvid's frame, Maryan's case study, both names on it. Gated behind both lists. Mutual list growth. | Maryan proposes after episode airs. |
| **Software Social** | One free month of the Core ($49) plan as a giveaway to 5 listeners selected by the hosts. Lets Michele/Colleen offer real value to their audience without asking for anything. | Maryan proposes in the pitch email if appropriate. |
| **Indie Hackers** | Original case study post on IH (not a cross-post — written specifically for the IH community) timed to drop the day the episode airs, driving traffic back to the podcast page. Plus a 30-day affiliate window for IH community members. | Maryan proposes after Courtland says yes. |
| **Build Your SaaS** | Integration tutorial: "How to use Transistor for the founder podcast tour" — written for Transistor's blog, credits Build Your SaaS. Sends Transistor relevant traffic. | Maryan offers in follow-up after recording. |
| **Startup Ideas (Greg)** | Featured case study in the diagnostic data report — Maryan publishes anonymized aggregate findings from the first 500 AI-shipped founders, credits Greg's "great flip" framing in the report's headline conclusion. Greg gets cited as the thesis originator in a piece of original research. | Maryan offers in the pitch as the post-episode deliverable. |

**Tracker rule:** every trade must be (a) concrete, (b) defensible — not vague "audience plug" energy, and (c) deliverable within 30 days of the episode airing. If it can't pass those three filters, replace it before sending the pitch.
