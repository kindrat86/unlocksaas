### §1.b Guest essay – "The Blank Offer Page" (free editorial variant)

**Length:** 612 words. Tuned for Indie Worldwide's typical guest-post length (500 to 700).
**Byline:** Maryan, founder, Unlock SaaS. Bio link in author byline (not in body).
**Editorial control:** Anthony retains full editorial control. Cuts to anywhere except the parable opener are accepted in advance.

---

```markdown
# The Blank Offer Page

I sat down one night to write the offer for a product I'd already shipped.

I had features for days. Traffic tactics for days. A list of social proof
ideas. A folder of competitor screenshots.

I opened a blank doc. Tried to write one sentence: who this is for, what it
does for them.

Forty minutes. Nothing.

That was the night I realized I'd been building a beautiful thing for no one
in particular.

---

I'm a non-engineer. I'm not a designer either. I am a marketer who never wrote
a line of production code. For most of my life that closed a door.

Then Lovable and Claude opened it. I shipped a dozen real products with AI
tools over the last year. The shipping felt like magic. What came after did
not.

Here's the part nobody warns the AI-built founder about.

You launch. You open Stripe. You sit there looking at a line that just lays
there. A handful of people wander in, poke around, drift back out. And you
reach for a story that lets you off the hook.

Mine was: "It needs one more feature."

Then: "The funnel's leaking."

Then I went embarrassingly deep into SEO, AEO, GEO. I got good at being found.
The line stayed flat. The truth I would not say: I was hiding.

Productive work is the best-camouflaged form of avoidance. Nobody – including
you – can call you out for it. I could have taught a class on schema markup.
I never wrote the offer.

---

What finally broke me wasn't the dashboard.

It was hearing my own voice come out of other people's mouths.

I sat with ten other founders shipping with AI tools last quarter. Half were
also-marketers. Half had no marketing background at all. The same flat line.
The same shelf of half-built products. The same frantic faith the next launch
would be the one.

Every conversation, somewhere around minute eighteen, a version of the same
sentence:

"I just need to ship one more thing."

Each of us had been building for an imagined audience – us, basically, with a
slightly different problem. None of us had pinned one real person and written
one specific promise to them.

I named the imaginary average composite, the way you name a pet you're keeping
at arm's length. Alex. I started writing as if Alex were the only person who
would ever read the page. The offer wrote itself in about two hours.

That was the work I'd skipped.

---

If you're shipping with Lovable or Claude or Cursor – or you're one of the
indie hackers in this community using nothing but Stripe Atlas and a Notion
doc – the work that gets you the first paying customer is not the work you
think it is.

It is not more features.

It is not more traffic.

It is one specific real person, one specific real promise, one specific real
message, and one Stripe charge that closes.

Everything else is decoration on the blank offer page.

I built a thing called Unlock SaaS for the next batch of founders who are
about to discover the same lesson the same way I did. As of writing this,
I have zero verified Stripe-cycle customers myself. The Verified Builder
ledger is public for that exact reason: I want my own first customer to land
on the same line as everyone else's. If you want a free version of the work,
the diagnostic at the URL in the byline labels what's broken in 90 seconds
and tells you the specific next step.

But honestly? The diagnostic isn't the point.

The point is the blank page.

Open it. Write the sentence. The product you've already shipped will quietly
become the answer to whatever you write next.

– Maryan
```

**Why this works for Anthony's list:**
- Indie Worldwide's audience is the closest one-to-one match for Alex. The "ten founders" passage is the mirror Anthony's readers want to hold up to themselves.
- The Embedded-Entrepreneur audience-first frame is honored (the "imagined audience" passage).
- The honest pre-revenue disclosure ("zero verified Stripe-cycle customers myself") puts the parable above the offer.
- Byline link is the only call to action. The body has zero promotional surface.

### §2.b Guest essay – "The Stripe Refresh"

**Length:** 618 words.

```markdown
# The Stripe Refresh

I knew I was in trouble the day I refreshed Stripe three times
before getting out of bed.

Not for any new reason. The line had been flat for eleven weeks.
I refreshed it because hope is the cheapest drug a non-engineer
founder has access to.

---

A year ago I shipped my first AI product with Lovable. Stripe = 0.
Three weeks later I shipped my second. Stripe = 0. By month four
I had four products live. Across all four products: two paying
customers. Both friends.

So I did what we all do.

I rewrote the homepage. Three times. I redesigned the pricing
page. Twice. I added a comparison table. I added a teaser video.
I added testimonials – the two friends, generous with their words.
I added a 7-day free trial. Then a 14-day free trial. Then I
removed the trial entirely.

The line stayed flat.

Then I did the founder version of going to graduate school: I
learned SEO. Real SEO, not the watered-down "publish a blog post"
version. Schema markup, internal-link graphs, hreflang, canonicals,
core web vitals, llms.txt. I built one of the cleanest indie
SaaS pages I have ever seen on the technical-SEO axis.

The line stayed flat.

---

What I would not say out loud, for nine months, was the truth that
sat under all of it.

I had never sat down and written to one real person.

Not in the marketing sense. In the literal sense. I had never
named one specific human being I was trying to help. I had never
written one specific promise to them. I had never sent the message.

I had built four products for "people who want to do X." Which is
the same as no one. The Stripe-refresh was not a sign of low
traffic. It was the math of my own avoidance, computed in real
time on a dashboard I wouldn't stop looking at.

---

The thing that broke it was not a new tool or a new framework.

It was hearing my own story back in someone else's mouth.

I sat with ten other founders shipping with AI tools last quarter.
Lovable people, Claude people, Cursor people. Two of them were
marketers like me. Three had no marketing background at all. Five
had backgrounds I never would have predicted.

Every one of them was refreshing Stripe more than they were
shipping. Every one of them was building for "people who want X."
Every one of them had a sales page that read like a list of
features instead of a letter to a real person.

When I heard the version of my own avoidance from a founder
two thousand miles away, with a totally different product, in a
totally different niche, I finally understood that the work I'd
been skipping was the work the entire cohort was skipping.

The work has a shape. It is small. It is not what you think it is.

It is naming one specific real person. Writing one specific real
promise to them. Sending one specific real message. Closing one
Stripe charge that proves the promise was real.

Everything before that – the SEO, the redesigns, the pricing
experiments, the trial-length tests – is the founder's
Stripe-refresh in different clothes.

---

I built a thing called Unlock SaaS for the next batch of founders
about to discover the same lesson. As of writing this, my own
Verified Builder ledger is empty. I have not closed my own first
verified Stripe cycle yet. The ledger is public for that exact
reason.

If you've been refreshing more than shipping, the diagnostic at
the URL in the byline labels what's broken in 90 seconds.

But you already know.

– Maryan
```

### §3.b Guest essay / podcast-pitch alt – "The Mirror in Ten Founders"

Arvid's medium is podcast-first; a guest essay for the newsletter is welcome but a podcast guest spot is the higher-leverage trade. The piece below works as either:

- Guest essay (Bootstrapped Founder Weekly): 620 words, byline link only
- Podcast guest-spot outline (Bootstrapped Founder podcast): the same content, expanded into a 25-minute conversation with the three sections becoming three episode beats

**Length (essay form):** 620 words.

---

```markdown
# The Mirror in Ten Founders

I built a dozen AI products with Lovable and Claude over the last
year. None of them have a paying customer.

That's not a hook. That's a state.

I read Zero to Sold in 2024. I nodded along. I told myself I had
internalized the Embedded Entrepreneur frame. Then I went and built
a dozen things for no one in particular anyway, because the
shipping itself was so seductive that I told myself the audience
would be a problem I'd solve later.

I am writing this because somebody needs to name the trap the
AI-shipped generation of founders is walking into, and the founders
who could name it are mostly too embarrassed to.

---

The trap has a shape.

When the shipping is hard, shipping IS the work. We learned that
from the bootstrap canon for twenty years. Build the thing. Get it
out the door. Sales is downstream of shipping.

When the shipping is easy, shipping is the avoidance.

I can ship a real working product in three weeks now. Not a
prototype. A real one, with Stripe, auth, a database, a deploy
pipeline, a documentation site. Three weeks. I have shipped twelve
of them.

What I have not done, even once, in a year, is sit down and write
to one real person.

The shipping has expanded to fill the space where the audience-
work used to live. It is so productive-feeling that I cannot
distinguish it from progress.

---

What finally broke it for me was not reading something. It was
sitting with ten other founders shipping the same way I was.

Two were marketers. Three had no marketing background. Five had
backgrounds I would not have predicted. Different niches. Different
tools – some Lovable, some Claude, some Cursor, some all three.
Different products at totally different stages.

Every one of them was doing the same thing.

Every one of them had a sales page that read like a list of
features. Every one of them was refreshing Stripe more than they
were shipping. Every one of them was telling themselves it was a
funnel problem or a feature problem or a pricing problem.

When I heard my own avoidance back in someone else's mouth, in a
totally different niche, with a totally different product, in a
totally different stage of their life – I finally understood that
the work the AI-shipped generation skips is not a personal failing.
It is the structural consequence of removing the friction that used
to force the audience-work.

We didn't skip the work because we're lazy. We skipped it because
nothing forces us to do it anymore.

---

The Embedded Entrepreneur frame's central insight – find your
people before they find you – is what reverses the trap. But the
mechanism is harder than the frame.

The mechanism is: you have to name one specific real person you
are trying to help, and you have to be willing to be wrong about
who that person is.

Most of us, when we try this, name a category instead of a
person. "Indie hackers." "AI-tool founders." "Solo-prenurs." These
are not people. These are flag-planting exercises that let us feel
like we did the work without doing the work.

A real person has a name. A real person can ignore your message.
A real person can be wrong about. A real person costs something
to name.

I built a thing called Unlock SaaS for the next batch of founders
about to discover this. As of writing, my own Verified Builder
ledger is empty. I have not closed my own first verified Stripe
cycle yet. The ledger is public for the same reason this essay
is honest: the work is the work, and pretending otherwise costs
you the year I just spent.

– Maryan
```

## §5 – Brunson Hard-Rule sanity check (per draft)

Pre-send checklist. Every draft above passes; this is the verification each time a NEW draft is added in a future cycle.

- [ ] No fabricated customer count. Each draft acknowledges N=0 explicitly OR is silent on count (never invents a number).
- [ ] No invented testimonials. No paraphrase of imaginary users.
- [ ] No promised future returns. The guarantee is stated verbatim as the offer term, never as a forecast.
- [ ] No slag of competitors. Lovable, Claude, Cursor named with respect.
- [ ] One link maximum. Body has zero links; byline has one.
- [ ] Sender identity locked. `maryan@unlocksaas.com`, signed "– Maryan."
- [ ] en-dashes only, no em-dashes.
- [ ] Voice reads like the X bio and the Indie Hackers profile (content-queue-week-1.md). If a stranger compared this draft to either of those, they'd say it's the same person.
- [ ] Specificity floor: at least one verbatim phrase from the target creator's recent canon is referenced (Reps 1-3) OR the parable is grounded in the same lived-experience details as content-queue-week-1.md (essays).
